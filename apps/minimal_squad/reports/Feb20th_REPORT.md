# CEPE-style Experiment Results: ModernBERT Encoder + TinyLlama Decoder

## Setup

All experiments use TinyLlama 1B as the pretrained decoder with cross-attention adapters injected between each layer's self-attention and FFN. Two data formats are compared:
- **C/Q//A**: context+question in encoder, answer generated by decoder
- **C//Q/A**: context-only in encoder, question+answer generated by decoder

## Key Findings

### 1. C/Q//A >> C//Q/A — the encoder must see the question

The most striking result: C/Q//A with CEPE reaches **77.6% EM** while C//Q/A with the same setup reaches only **16.4% EM** — a 61-point gap. Even full fine-tuning of all 1.87B params only brings C//Q/A to 18.0%. When the encoder sees only context without the question, it produces question-blind representations that the cross-attention adapters cannot compensate for. The decoder must receive question-aware encoder states to generate correct answers.

### 2. CEPE matches full fine-tuning with half the trainable params (C/Q//A)

`400m_cepe` (77.6% EM, 766M trainable) essentially ties `400m_finetune` (78.4% EM, 1,866M trainable). Fine-tuning the encoder while keeping the decoder frozen is sufficient — the decoder's pretrained language modeling capability doesn't need task-specific adaptation.

### 3. Adapters alone are not enough

`400m_frozen` (43.4% EM on C/Q//A) is ~35 points behind CEPE (77.6% EM). The cross-attention adapters can bridge encoder and decoder, but without encoder fine-tuning the encoder representations aren't task-adapted enough for strong performance.

### 4. Encoder size matters significantly (C/Q//A)

Across both freeze strategies, 400M consistently outperforms 150M:
- Frozen: 43.4 vs 22.4 (+21 EM)
- CEPE: 77.6 vs 55.6 (+22 EM)

The gap stays roughly constant, suggesting encoder capacity is a bottleneck regardless of training strategy.

### 5. C//Q/A results are uniformly poor

All C//Q/A experiments stay below 18% EM regardless of training mode or encoder size. Notably, 150M_frozen (14.2%) actually outperforms 400M_frozen (8.0%) on C//Q/A — the opposite of C/Q//A — suggesting that without the question, a larger encoder doesn't help and may even overfit to context-only representations.

### 6. Overfitting appears at epoch 3-4

Both `400m_cepe` and `400m_finetune` peak at epoch 3 on C/Q//A, then degrade. The 150M experiments are still improving at epoch 5, suggesting smaller models need more training.

## Takeaway

The CEPE strategy (`--pretrained_weight_updating 0.0 --encoder_weight_updating 0.333`) with **C/Q//A format** is the recommended default for pretrained decoder experiments: it matches full fine-tuning quality while training only 41% of parameters and preserving decoder weights. The C//Q/A format is not viable — it caps at ~18% EM regardless of model size or training strategy.
